## 1. Why people aren't buying

Your ads are doing their job (6.5–7% CTR, 14% ATC — genuinely strong). Your buyer is doing his job: he reads deep, adds to cart, clicks chat. Then he hits the cart, spends a median 24 seconds, and 89.3% leave without touching checkout.

Here is what happens in those 24 seconds. He's about to send ~AED 4,000 to a company he'd never heard of an hour ago. So he does what every 35–54-year-old man with money does at the moment of payment: he stops feeling and starts auditing. And the first thing the audit finds is this:

> "ONLY AED 3,990 TODAY" / "AED 1,500 OFF APPLIED | OFFER ENDS FRIDAY" / **"Regular price AUD 1,505 | Sale price AUD 1,505"**

The crossed-out price equals the sale price. The "AED 1,500 OFF" is visibly not applied to anything. And the currency has silently switched from dirhams to Australian dollars on a Dubai store. In the exact moment the buyer is asking *"is this company real?"*, the page answers: **the discount is fake and we don't know what country we're in.**

That's the core diagnosis: **this is not a desire problem, it's a verification failure.** The page is fluent at selling the feeling ("switched on") and mute on every question a high-ticket buyer asks at checkout: who are you, does this machine actually exist, will it arrive, what happens when it breaks in my bathroom in Dubai. Three times more people click chat than checkout (19 vs 6). Read that literally: **your buyers are asking to talk to a human before wiring you money, and nobody built that path.** The 75 dead clicks say the same thing — people are clicking images and spec text hoping for proof (video, zoom, real photos) and getting nothing back.

The returning-visitor data confirms it: people who come back convert to cart at 4x the rate of first-timers. They *want* to buy — they just need a second session to talk themselves into trusting you. And you have zero mechanism (0 email subscribers, no retargeting) to bring them back. You're relying on them remembering you.

## 2. Messaging teardown

**The pricing block — the single most damaging element on the page.**
Three currencies across the funnel (€ in old ad copy, AED in the pitch, AUD at the money moment). A discount that mathematically doesn't exist. "TODAY" and "ENDS FRIDAY" stacked — double fake urgency. Any buyer who checks — and at this price they all check — concludes the discount is theatre, and then re-reads your entire page through that lens. Your 30-day guarantee is now issued by a company that fakes discounts.
**Replace with:** `AED 3,990 — includes wall mount, power kit, and flow upgrade (AED 350 value). July batch ships July 15.` One price. One currency. If you want an offer, make it a real, dated launch offer: "Founding batch pricing — first 25 Dubai units."

**"Ships July 15 | July Dubai batch almost full."**
You've sold 2 units in 90 days. "Almost full" is a claim the buyer can't verify but can smell. Scarcity from an unknown brand reads as pressure; pressure from a stranger reads as scam.
**Replace with:** `July batch: [X] of 25 units remaining` — with a real number you update, or `Small-batch production. July allocation ships July 15; after that, September.` True, specific, calm.

**The variant "reviews."**
> "The free flow upgrade makes the offer easier to decide on, but checkout needs to feel fast."

No customer in human history has said this. It's CRO meeting notes wearing a five-star costume. Same for "People are choosing KRYO for the reset, not the ritual" — that's a positioning line, not feedback. And "3-person feedback sample" is an apology label. **Fake-sounding reviews at AED 3,990 are worse than no reviews** — reviews are the one element buyers actively stress-test.
**Replace with:** kill the section until you have two real customers on camera or by name ("Ahmed, Dubai Marina, unit #2 — photo of it in his bathroom"). Until then, run a founder note: "We've shipped [N] units. I'm the founder; here's my WhatsApp. Ask the awkward questions."

**Feature-card language.**
"Immersive experience," "Ready inside your routine," "Made for inner-city living" — three of six cards are adjectives with nothing to audit. The buyer's actual questions — *how cold on my skin, how long does 1°C take from turn-on, what does 0.1HP sound like in an ensuite at 5:45am, what does 3–4 sessions per 12L refill mean for a couple* — are unanswered or buried in a spec table. Note the internal tension a sharp buyer will spot: the ad promise is "on demand," the spec says sessions-per-refill. Answer it before he asks.
**Replace three cards with proof cards:** measured cool-down time (on video), noise level (phone dB app on film — imperfect but honest), refill/session math in plain words.

**The guarantee.**
> "If it does not help you move from half-awake to switched on, we'll collect it and refund"

The condition is a feeling — subjective, sounds like an escape hatch (who judges "switched on"?). High-ticket guarantees must be mechanical.
**Replace with:** `30 days in your bathroom. Any reason, or no reason: WhatsApp us, we collect it from your door within 5 days, full refund to your card. You pay nothing for the return.` Then state who honors it: a name, a Dubai contact. An anonymous guarantee is worth its signature — currently: none.

**Brand-voice whiplash.**
Ads: "Unlock 530% More Focus" (fabricated-sounding stat — kill it today), "Plunge is Dead," "Superhuman Focus," leftover "NUE Shower" € pre-order copy. Page: measured, domestic, Dubai-specific. The buyer clicks a biohacker-hype ad, lands on a home-appliance page, and the brand has been NUE, EverestPod, and KRYO across the creative library. Each seam is a "something's off" tick. The page voice — "Built for Dubai," no-nail rail, setup section — is the right one. Make the ads sound like the page, retire every NUE/€ creative, pick one name forever.

## 3. The trust gap at AED 3,990 — the buyer's unspoken objections

1. **"Who is this company?"** No founder, no face, no address, no phone, no "about," no registration. (First thing he Googles — what does he find?)
2. **"Does the product physically exist?"** Renders and copy, no water moving. The demo video is the single most valuable asset in the pipeline.
3. **"If I pay, does anything arrive?"** Card-not-present, four grand, unknown entity, "pre-order" energy. Payment protection? COD or deposit option? (In Dubai, COD is the trust default for unknown sellers.)
4. **"When it breaks, who fixes it?"** A compressor appliance in a bathroom. Warranty length? Local service?
5. **"Is it safe in a bathroom?"** Electricity + water + kids. IPX4 sits naked in a spec table with no plain-language explanation.
6. **"Why is the discount fake?"** — retroactively poisons answers 1–5.

The page currently answers zero of these. Every dirham of ad spend buys traffic that arrives, asks these six questions, gets silence, and leaves.

## 4. Top 5 plays, $0–30/day, ranked

**1. Fix the money moment (today, $0, 2 hours).** One currency (AED), one honest price, real batch count, mechanical guarantee wording, founder name + WhatsApp on the page. Removing the anti-conversion; everything else compounds only after this. **Proof in 2 weeks:** cart→checkout off 7.2%; cart dwell above 24s.

**2. Weaponize the chat signal — founder-answered WhatsApp (this week, $0).** 19 chat clicks vs 6 checkout clicks: chat *is* the checkout. WhatsApp button — "Questions? WhatsApp Tom, the founder — replies in minutes" — answered personally; offer a 10-minute live video demo on the call. Every conversation is free objection research. **Proof:** chats opened, chat→sale (one closed chat pays a month of ad spend).

**3. Demo video as the hero, not a section (this week, $0 — already filming).** Founder on camera, face visible: turn on, fill, run, hand under the water, react to the cold. First 3 seconds above the fold, and cut into ads. Answers objections #1 and #2 simultaneously; the one thing stock-footage competitors can't fake. **Proof:** play rate, dead-clicks falling, first-visit ATC.

**4. Capture loop for the deliberators (week 2, ~$0).** Returning visitors reach cart at 4x; only 8% return; nobody is captured. Exit/cart-abandon prompt — not a discount (fake discounting just got deleted) — "Get the full demo video + spec sheet + my direct WhatsApp" for an email. 3-email sequence: demo → guarantee/service explained → founder story + real batch deadline. Optionally a refundable AED 200 reservation — a small yes before the big one. **Proof:** subscribers >0, return rate off 8%, first email-attributed sale.

**5. Radical-transparency proof block replacing fake reviews (week 2, $0).** "We're new. Here's who we are" — founder photo, workshop, unit #1 in the founder's own bathroom, return process in 3 steps, warranty in plain words, founding-customer offer for the first 10 Dubai buyers (personal install call included). Unknown brands don't win by imitating incumbent proof; they win by making newness the story. **Proof:** section dwell, chat questions shifting from "are you real?" to "which bathroom wall?"

## 5. Verdict on the planned split test

**Do not send a single dirham of traffic as configured. Invalid twice, mistargeted once.**

1. **Variant says "Status: Sold out"** — testing "can buy" vs "cannot buy." Voids the test alone.
2. **Variant shows AUD 1,505 / AUD 2,790 crossed out** — wrong currency AND a different fake-discount story than control: multiple variables, untestable.
3. **The written reviews will hurt, not help** — a trust penalty on the variant; a loss teaches nothing.
4. **Wrong battlefield** — the product page works (14% ATC); the cart is where 89.3% die. Pricing/trust fixes belong on BOTH pages or the kill zone eats every variant equally.
5. **Statistical reality** — at ~$25/day and 2 sales/90d, a purchase-metric split test won't reach significance this year. Judge on cart→checkout clicks and WhatsApp conversations; treat as directional.

**What to actually run:** fix pricing, sold-out status, currency, guarantee wording on both pages first (not hypotheses; broken is broken). Then test ONE variable: **founder demo video above the fold** vs control. Delete the three written reviews entirely. The video is the only element that answers the question the buyer is actually asking — *"is this real?"* — and right now that's the only test worth paying for.
